# Subscription metrics foundation

[![ci](https://github.com/fred1433/subscription-metrics-foundation/actions/workflows/ci.yml/badge.svg)](https://github.com/fred1433/subscription-metrics-foundation/actions/workflows/ci.yml)

A small, runnable dbt and MCP example of a trusted metrics layer for a UK cat-food subscription business, shaped on the public subscription flow of [Untamed](https://untamed.com): 12-tin trial, first box 9 days later, then every 28 days, Change Date, "I need it now", pauses, payment retries.

> **Synthetic data only.** Every row, price and volume is generated by [`generator/generate.py`](generator/generate.py) with a fixed seed. No Untamed data was used or seen, and nothing below is a statement about Untamed's numbers. The mechanisms come from Untamed's public help centre and terms, each one linked in [Where each mechanism comes from](#where-each-mechanism-comes-from); everything else is marked as an assumption. Not affiliated with Untamed.

## Five things in your own flow that quietly bend the numbers

Each one is public, linked, and handled explicitly in this repository.

| Your mechanism | What it does to a naive report | How it is handled here |
|---|---|---|
| A paid [12-tin trial](https://untamed.gorgias.help/en-GB/the-reason-behind-our-12-tin-trial-1810484), first box [9 days later, then every 28 days](https://untamed.gorgias.help/en-GB/how-billing-works-1526817) | "Conversion" depends on when you look: a trial bought yesterday is not a failure; 13 renewals a year fall into 12 months unevenly | conversion over a declared 42-day window, only for trials whose window has closed; renewals tracked by expected date, not calendar month |
| [Change Date](https://untamed.gorgias.help/en-GB/changing-your-renewal-date-564963) and ["I need it now"](https://untamed.gorgias.help/en-GB/using-the-i-need-it-now-feature-1760339) (order now, next renewal 28 days later) | a late box looks like churn, an early one like an extra customer | they move the expected renewal date and never the subscription state |
| Failed payment [retried for 30 days](https://untamed.com/pages/terms) (terms 7.6) | a failure counts as a lost subscriber, or a retry as a sale | state `payment_retry`: not churn, not revenue until paid |
| [Blank order](https://untamed.gorgias.help/en-GB/updating-your-payment-details-why-you-might-see-a-blank-order-1821149) when a card is updated | inflates order counts and depresses average order value | classified by its motif and removed from commercial orders, never by its 0 amount |
| [73p card check](https://untamed.gorgias.help/en-GB/why-have-i-been-charged-73p-1810773), refunded | appears as a charge and a refund in payment data | modelled as a reversed card check: never revenue, listed in the gross-to-net check |

The deeper problem: subscription orders start in your platform and (assumed) end up in Shopify, then in the warehouse, so two systems describe the same order and can disagree; and an AI agent pointed at the warehouse will write its own definition of churn unless it can only ask for governed metrics. All tables in sections 1 to 4 are written by [`scripts/readme_results.py`](scripts/readme_results.py) from the run; CI fails if this README and the run disagree.

## 1. One customer, day by day

Synthetic dates, J0 = 1 June 2026. The trial is paid on J0, the first box is due on J9, the customer moves it to J20 on J5, updates their card on J6 (which creates a blank order and a 73p check), the J20 payment fails and the retry succeeds on J23. The state is rebuilt from what was known each evening, so J19 never uses the J23 success.

<!-- generated:journey -->
| Day | Date | Events that day | State known that evening | First box paid | Next renewal expected |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| J0 | 2026-06-01 | trial_purchased, subscription_enrolled | trial_pending_first_box | no | 2026-06-10 |
| J5 | 2026-06-06 | renewal_date_changed | trial_pending_first_box | no | 2026-06-21 |
| J6 | 2026-06-07 | payment_method_updated | trial_pending_first_box | no | 2026-06-21 |
| J19 | 2026-06-20 | (none) | trial_pending_first_box | no | 2026-06-21 |
| J20 | 2026-06-21 | payment_failed | payment_retry | no | 2026-06-21 |
| J21 | 2026-06-22 | (none) | payment_retry | no | 2026-06-21 |
| J23 | 2026-06-24 | payment_succeeded, first_subscription_box_paid | active | yes | 2026-07-19 |

The J6 blank order lands as `blank_payment_update`, commercial = false. Its 73p card check is reversed and listed in the gross-to-net check.
<!-- /generated:journey -->

A moved date is not churn and a payment in retry is not churn. When the 30-day retries end, the unpaid order is cancelled; that the subscription itself stays alive is an assumption (terms 7.6 only speak about orders). A subscription whose renewal is more than 35 days overdue with nothing known since is `lapsed` and stops counting as active.

## How it is built

```
sources (platform, shopify, ads, source_export)
  -> staging         one view per source table, typed, deduplicated on the source key, UTC instants
  -> intermediate    identity links, review queue, households, state changes, trial eligibility
  -> marts           dim_customers, fct_orders, fct_revenue_movements, fct_trials,
                     fct_subscription_state_daily, fct_ad_spend_daily
  -> reconciliation  rec_order_count, rec_completeness, rec_gross_to_net, rec_ad_spend,
                     rec_trial_eligibility, rec_exceptions, rec_summary
  -> metrics         metric_registry, source_freshness, metric_quality, metric_values
  -> MCP server      list_metrics, query_metric (reads metric_values, metric_registry, metric_quality only)
```

### Identity: customers, accounts, households and cats are different things

- **Customer** (`dim_customers`): one row per customer. A platform account and a Shopify customer are the same customer only through an explicit cross-reference (assumed field `accounts.shopify_customer_id`). Each link keeps `link_rule` and `link_evidence`.
- **Never merged on email.** Three normalisations are computed and kept apart: lower-case, `+alias` removed, dots removed from the local part. They only fill a review queue (`int_identity_candidates`, status `unresolved`).
- **Household** (`int_probable_households`): customers sharing a normalised delivery address are a *possible* household; with the same payment fingerprint (assumed field) a *probable* one. Untamed's terms (clause 15.3) say email, payment method and delivery address may be matched to decide **referral eligibility**; that clause does not define an analytical identity, so here it only feeds an eligibility review.
- **Cats** are a plan attribute (`cats_on_plan`), not an entity.

| Fixture | Expected | Test |
|---|---|---|
| Email changed through customer care, Shopify kept the old one | 1 customer, whole history attached | `tests/identity/email_change_keeps_one_customer.sql` |
| Two flatmates, same address written differently, own cards | 2 customers, 1 possible household, no flag | `tests/identity/shared_address_two_customers_one_household.sql` |
| Second trial, same card and address, new email | exception on the later trial, both orders kept | `tests/identity/second_household_trial_flagged_and_kept.sql` |
| `Ella.Hart+untamed@` vs `ellahart@` | 2 customers, 1 unresolved candidate | `tests/identity/normalised_email_never_merges.sql` |
| Emails differ only by case, cross-reference present | linked by the reference, not the email | `tests/identity/case_variant_linked_by_xref.sql` |

### Lifecycle and churn

`fct_subscription_state_daily` holds the state of every subscription on every day, as known that evening (event time and ingestion time are kept apart). States: `trial_pending_first_box`, `active`, `payment_retry` (last payment failed 30 days ago or fewer), `lapsed` (renewal more than 35 days overdue, nothing known since: excluded from active, investigated rather than called churn), `paused`, `cancelled`. Change Date, skip and "I need it now" move `expected_renewal_date` and nothing else. Churn itself stays a contract (below) until the period and the treatment of long pauses are agreed.

### Metrics: one registry, implemented or contract

The registry lives in one place, [`macros/metric_registry.sql`](macros/metric_registry.sql) (plain YAML inside a dbt macro). dbt materialises it as `metric_registry`; the MCP server reads that table and holds no copy. Each entry declares id, version, definition, grain, numerator, denominator, unit, allowed slices, minimum group size, maturity policy, source dependencies and required quality status.

| Metric | Status | Definition (one sentence, from the registry) |
|---|---|---|
| `trial_conversion_rate` | implemented | Share of trials whose first subscription box was paid within the conversion window, counted only over trials whose window had fully closed at the cutoff; cancelled trials stay in the denominator. |
| `net_revenue` | implemented | Order totals after discounts booked on the order date, minus refunds actually paid out booked on the refund date (Europe/London), excluding blank orders; card checks are never revenue. |
| `active_subscriptions` | implemented | Subscriptions whose first box has been paid and whose state at the end of the month, as known that day, is active or in payment retry; paused, cancelled, lapsed (renewal more than 35 days overdue) and trial-only subscriptions are excluded. |
| `ad_spend_per_trial` | implemented | Meta, Google and TikTok spend for the month, deduplicated on the full source key, divided by trials purchased in the same month; a blended DTC figure, not a channel CAC. |
| `subscription_churn_rate` | contract | Missing: calendar month or 28-day cycle, treatment of long pauses, reactivations. |
| `retention_by_trial_cohort` | contract | Missing: agreed meaning of active, cohort basis. |
| `observed_net_revenue_180d` | contract | Observed only, never predicted. Missing: add-on orders in or out, retail in or out. |
| `cac` | contract | Missing: agency and creative costs, attribution mapping, denominator. |
| `contribution_margin` | contract | Missing: cost per SKU, fulfilment and courier, payment fees, accounting mapping. |
| `payback_period` | contract | From the trial, non-converted trials included. Missing: contribution margin and CAC. |

Conventions: amounts are integer pence; a refund reduces revenue when the money leaves, once, whatever the connector redelivers; discounts (trial promo, referral) are netted from revenue once and are not added again to an acquisition cost; rates combine as sum of numerators over sum of denominators, never as an average of rates.

**Scope note.** Untamed also sells through grocers and pet retailers ([source](https://untamed.gorgias.help/en-GB/can-i-buy-untamed-food-in-stores-1810688)). Every acquisition figure here is DTC only; retail is out of scope and no claim is made about its effect.

### Reconciliation: five separate checks

| Check | Source side | Warehouse side | Rules |
|---|---|---|---|
| `order_count` | Shopify orders landed | commercial orders in `fct_orders` | blank orders leave the count by motif; a free replacement stays |
| `completeness` | independent export of paid platform orders, taken before the landing fault was injected | landed Shopify orders carrying a platform id | missing and unexpected keys, amount per key, same currency and cutoff, 6-hour arrival tolerance; a scheduled order not yet charged is not missing revenue |
| `gross_to_net` | Shopify cash: sales minus refunds actually paid | `fct_revenue_movements` | lines minus discounts must equal order totals row by row; pending refunds and card checks listed beside |
| `ad_spend` | the ad platforms' own daily totals | spend deduplicated on platform, account, campaign and date | never deduplicated on platform and day |
| `trial_eligibility` | trial events | `fct_trials` | a second trial in a probable household is flagged and kept: it is a real sale |

This is a demonstration of the controls on synthetic data, not a reconciliation of Untamed's data.

### MCP server

[`mcp_server/server.py`](mcp_server/server.py), official Python SDK, stdio transport, read-only DuckDB connection.

- **Disclosure guardrails (implemented):** aggregates only from `metric_values`, which holds no personal data; slices from the registry's allow-list; cells under the minimum group size (10) suppressed, with complementary suppression (when a month of a split has one hidden cell, its smallest remaining cell is hidden too); range totals only on the unsplit series and only when no month in the range is hidden; pending counts only on the unsplit series; at most 24 months and 150 rows per call; every identifier in the SQL is fixed, every value is a bound parameter; every call appended to `logs/mcp_calls.jsonl`, and no answer is given if the log cannot be written.
- **Access control (not implemented here):** who may call the server at all. On BigQuery: a service account that can read only a `metrics` dataset, plus authentication on the MCP endpoint itself.
- **Freshness:** a metric whose source is older than its tolerated age at the cutoff answers `unavailable` with the reason, never a stale number.

### Time zones

Orders and events are stored as UTC instants with a Europe/London business date derived from them. Ad accounts are taken to report daily spend in Europe/London dates (an assumption to confirm per account). Tests cover local midnight and both clock changes.

### Warehouse target

What runs where, exactly:

- **Executed on DuckDB** (locally and in CI): the dbt project, the harness, the MCP server and every Python test. The harness, the MCP server and the tests read DuckDB files directly; they are DuckDB-bound.
- **Compiled for BigQuery, not executed**: CI compiles every model, test and analysis with dbt-bigquery 1.12.1 against a fake project, without any connection ([`ci/profiles.yml`](ci/profiles.yml)), then [`ci/check_bigquery_sql.py`](ci/check_bigquery_sql.py) parses every compiled file with sqlglot's BigQuery dialect and rejects DuckDB-only functions and DATETIME arithmetic (`dbt.dateadd` compiles to `datetime_add`, which BigQuery refuses to compare with a TIMESTAMP; timestamps and dates use `ts_add_days`, `ts_add_hours`, `date_add_days` instead). That catches syntax and the known type trap; it does not prove the queries run, return the same numbers or cost what they should.
- **Not done**: a `dbt build` on BigQuery and a comparison of `rec_summary` and `metric_values` with the DuckDB run. That is the first step on real infrastructure.

Engine differences sit in [`macros/cross_db.sql`](macros/cross_db.sql) (`london_date`, `day_series`, `regex_replace_all`, timestamp parsing and arithmetic, `type_double`), alongside dbt's own cross-database macros (`dbt.date_trunc`, `dbt.datediff`, `dbt.hash`, `dbt.type_*`). [`profiles.yml.example`](profiles.yml.example) holds a BigQuery target. The Shopify tables `order`, `order_tag`, `order_line_items`, `customer` and `transaction` and the `_weld_synced` column follow [Weld's documented Shopify schema](https://weld.app/docs/applications/shopify); `refund` and `order_line_refund` are assumed names, and which tables are enabled in your workspace is a discovery item.

### Assumptions (to verify before building anything real)

- The subscription platform pushes paid orders to Shopify and writes its own order id into `source_identifier`. Payment options such as Shop Pay ([source](https://untamed.gorgias.help/en-GB/paying-with-shop-pay-1784942)) suggest Shopify is involved; the exact flow is the first thing to check in Weld.
- Every platform table and column name, including `shopify_customer_id` and `payment_fingerprint`.
- Event names (`skipped`, `expedited`, `payment_retry_exhausted`, `first_subscription_box_paid`...) and their exact semantics.
- After a successful retry, the next renewal stays at the original due date plus 28 days.
- The blank order also reaches Shopify, as a zero order with its motif.
- How the 73p check and its reversal are recorded (here: the platform's payment table).
- Shopify table names `refund` and `order_line_refund`, and the columns of `order_line_items`.
- When retries end, the subscription stays alive and its next renewal is the first 28-day step after the retry window.
- Prices, volumes, recipes and names: invented.
